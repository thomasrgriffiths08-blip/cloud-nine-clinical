A

How to complain

Most concerns are best raised as soon as they happen, with the person you were dealing with. If that does not put it right, or you would rather not, contact our complaints manager, {{COMPLAINTS_MANAGER}}:

Someone else can complain for you, such as a relative or an advocate, with your written consent.

Please complain within 12 months of what happened, or of when you found out about it. We can still look at an older complaint if it is possible to investigate it fairly.

B

What happens next

  1. We acknowledge your complaint within {{ACK_DAYS}} working days, and offer to talk it through with you.
  2. We investigate. Anything about your clinical care is reviewed by a senior clinician who was not involved in it.
  3. We answer in writing within {{RESPONSE_DAYS}} working days: what we found, what we are doing about it, and an apology where we got it wrong. If it will take longer, we tell you why and when to expect our answer.
  4. We offer to meet, if you would find that helpful, to go through our answer with you.

Every complaint is reviewed, with names removed, for what it can teach us, and we change how we work where it shows we should.

C

If you are not satisfied: an independent review

If you are not happy with our final answer, you can ask for an independent review. Which service reviews it depends on who paid for your care.

If you paid for your care privately

You can ask {{INDEPENDENT_REVIEW_BODY}} to review how we handled your complaint. For independent clinics this is usually the Independent Sector Complaints Adjudication Service: iscas.org.uk.

If your care was funded by the NHS

You can ask the Parliamentary and Health Service Ombudsman to look at your complaint once our own procedure has finished: ombudsman.org.uk, or call 0345 015 4033.

D

The Care Quality Commission

The clinic is registered with the Care Quality Commission, {{CQC_NUMBER}}. The CQC does not investigate individual complaints, but it wants to hear about your care, good or bad, and uses what people tell it to decide when and where to inspect.

Tell the CQC about your care at cqc.org.uk/give-feedback-on-care, or call 03000 616161.

E

Concerns about a doctor

If you are worried that a doctor is not fit to practise, you can raise it with the General Medical Council, which registers every doctor who works in the UK. You do not need to complain to us first.

F

Help to complain

You do not have to do this alone. Your local Healthwatch can tell you about free, independent advocacy services near you: healthwatch.co.uk.

If you need this procedure in another format, or need an interpreter, tell us and we will arrange it. See the accessibility statement.
